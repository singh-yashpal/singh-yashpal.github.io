Research

01 / ELECTRONIC-STRUCTURE METHODS

Self-interaction correction and localized orbitals

Self-interaction error can distort the description of localized electronic states, stretched bonds, and transition states. My work examines these error sources and develops methods within the Fermi-Löwdin orbital self-interaction correction (FLO-SIC) framework for periodic materials.

A central direction is a Wannier-first approach that constructs localized, Wannier-like orbitals directly in real space using Gaussian basis functions, reducing dependence on conventional Block-state workflows.

Research directions

  • Extend periodic FLOSIC to magnets, chiral, helical, and low-symmetry systems.
  • Evaluate Perdew-Zunger self-interaction corrections for stretched bonds, charged defects, and localized excitations.
  • Develop automated workflows and benchmark datasets for systematic validation.
02 / CATALYSIS AND ENERGY

Reliable reaction energetics

I use first-principles calculations to investigate catalytic mechanisms and structure-property relationships in transition-metal phosphides and carbides, single-atom catalysts, and doped two-dimensional materials. Applications include oxygen reduction, oxygen evolution, hydrogen evolution, and selective hydrogenation.

Reaction barriers involving stretched bonds and hydrogen transfer provide a connection between electronic-structure accuracy and catalytic predictions.

Research directions

  • Screen transition-metal, bimetallic, and 2D catalyst platforms using DFT and targeted self-interaction corrections.
  • Identify chemical regimes where localized states and stretched-bond intermediates require improved electronic-structure treatments.
  • Explore solvent and electrolyte effects using ab initio molecular dynamics and quantum continuum approaches.
03 / OPTOELECTRONICS

Electronic and optical response

My research links electronic structure to optical behavior in perovskites and related semiconductors, hybrid halides, and donor-acceptor conjugated polymers. Collaboration with experimental groups connects calculations to materials selection and the interpretation of optical measurements.

Research directions

  • Investigate lead-free and double perovskites and related semiconductors.
  • Use GW and Bethe-Salpeter equation methods to study absorption, excitons, and emission.
  • Study defects, stability, and quantum confinement in optoelectronic materials.
04 / MANY-BODY THEORY

Self-consistent GW theory

It is now well known that traditional Kohn-Sham (KS) density functional theory (DFT) with local exchange-correlation (xc) functionals such as LDA and GGA underestimates band gaps because of incomplete cancellation of the self-interaction energy and the absence of long-range polarization effects. Moreover, KS eigenvalues cannot be used to explain observed photoemission spectroscopy and optical absorption, even though they are often used as the starting point in GW calculations.

The single-shot flavor of the GW approximation, G0W0, is quite successful in predicting experimental band gaps of weakly correlated s-p bonded systems. However, because it lacks self-consistency, results depend on the choice of starting xc functional and can vary significantly across the literature.

Several fully self-consistent GW methods, including scGW and QSGW approaches, have been explored with partial success. These methods often systematically underestimate dielectric constants, leading to overestimated band gaps. To improve the description of electronic spectra, it is necessary to go beyond the conventional GW approximation by incorporating vertex corrections in both the self-energy and the screened interaction W. A graphical representation of Hedin's equations with vertex terms is a natural framework for this extension, with vertex corrections corresponding to exchange of random-phase approximation bubble diagrams.

Attempts to partially include vertex corrections are being pursued through forms such as second-order screened exchange (SOSEX) and related approximations, which can improve band-gap predictions. A full self-consistent solution of Hedin's equations remains technically challenging, and computational efficiency is often improved through strategies such as diagonal G and low-rank or subspace W approximations.

Tagged work: Analysis of diagonal G and subspace W approximations within fully self-consistent GW calculations for bulk semiconducting systems.

Many-body theory and scalable computation

My background includes relativistic coupled-cluster calculations for atomic properties and electric dipole moments, alongside self-consistent GW methods for semiconductors. Across these areas, I focus on balancing electronic-structure accuracy with memory use and computational cost.
