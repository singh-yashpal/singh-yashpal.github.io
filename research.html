01 / ELECTRONIC-STRUCTURE METHODS

Self-interaction correction and localized orbitals

Self-interaction error can distort the description of localized electronic states, stretched bonds, and transition states. My work examines these error sources and develops methods within density-functional theory to improve the treatment of localized electrons.

A central direction is a Wannier-first approach that constructs localized, Wannier-like orbitals directly in real space using Gaussian basis functions, reducing dependence on conventional post-processing procedures.

FEATURED PAPER

A Wannier-first formulation for extended systems

This real-space formulation constructs variational Wannier-like functions self-consistently within a finite Wannier domain. The orbitals provide the occupied space and are used to build the density and self-consistent field.

The framework naturally accommodates combined translation–rotation (screw) symmetries, making it suitable for chiral and helical systems with finite twist angles.

Read the published paper in The Journal of Chemical Physics (2026), DOI: 10.1063/5.0348813

02 / REACTION BARRIERS

Analyzing PZ-SIC beyond the LSDA

Incorporating self-interaction corrections can substantially improve density-functional predictions of chemical reaction barrier heights. In this work, I performed an orbital-by-orbital analysis of Perdew–Zunger self-interaction corrections across the LSDA, PBE, and SCAN functionals.

The analysis follows the reactant (R), transition-state (TS), and product (P) configurations. Across all three functionals, the dominant contribution to the SIC of barrier heights comes from orbitals whose localization changes along the reaction coordinate.

XC/H as an orbital diagnostic

The ratio of self-exchange–correlation energy to self-Hartree energy, XC/H, measures the one-electron self-interaction error of an orbital.

XC/H = 1.0 means exact cancellation and therefore no contribution to the FLOSIC correction. Larger values are associated with stretched or strongly lobed orbitals.

Presentation highlights

Read the published JCP paper: “The rise and fall of stretched bond errors: Extending the analysis of Perdew–Zunger self-interaction corrections of reaction barrier heights beyond the LSDA.”

03 / CATALYSIS AND ENERGY

Electrochemistry and electrocatalysis

My research in sustainable energy focuses on the development of advanced nanomaterials for electrocatalysis. In particular, I have investigated single-atom catalysts in which isolated metal atoms are stabilized on two-dimensional supports.

Through extensive screening of metal- and non-metal-doped 2D materials, I have evaluated promising single-atom electrocatalysts for the oxygen reduction reaction (ORR) and oxygen evolution reaction (OER).

ORR and OER are central to proton-exchange-membrane fuel cells, water splitting, and metal–air batteries, but their sluggish kinetics often require scarce and expensive noble metals.

Computational approaches

  • Plane-wave density-functional theory using VASP and JDFTx.
  • Atomic Simulation Environment (ASE) workflows with climbing-image nudged elastic band (CI-NEB) calculations for kinetic barriers.
  • Q-Chem calculations for complementary molecular and electronic-structure analysis.
  • Continuum solvation models, including VASPsol, CANDLE, and SALSA, for solvent effects in electrochemical reactions.

Electrochemical mechanisms

My ongoing work examines water-solvent effects on ORR and OER for promising metal-doped carbon nanomaterials such as Fe–N–C. Using continuum solvation models and the grand-canonical approach, this work connects electrode potential, solvent response, and reaction kinetics.

Research directions

  • Screen transition-metal, bimetallic, and 2D catalyst platforms using DFT and targeted self-interaction corrections.
  • Identify chemical regimes where localized states and stretched-bond intermediates require improved electronic-structure treatments.
  • Model explicit and continuum solvent effects, electrode potential, metal dissolution, and electrochemical reaction kinetics.
04 / GW THEORY AND OPTOELECTRONICS

Electronic and optical response

My research links electronic structure to optical behavior in perovskites and related semiconductors, hybrid halides, donor–acceptor conjugated polymers, and low-dimensional luminescent materials. Collaboration with experimental groups connects first-principles predictions to synthesis, spectroscopy, and device-relevant behavior.

Hybrid halides and perovskite-related materials

The role of composition and dimensionality is central to tuning band-edge states, excitonic behavior, optical properties, and stability in hybrid halide materials.

Pivotal role of A-site cations in tailoring the band-edge states, optical properties, and stability of 0D hybrid indium chlorides. This study shows how A-site cations influence the electronic structure, optical response, and stability of zero-dimensional hybrid indium chlorides, providing a route to compositionally tune light absorption and emission.

Read the Chemistry of Materials paper (2022), DOI: 10.1021/acs.chemmater.2c02737

Donor–acceptor conjugated polymers

Electronic properties and optical spectra of donor–acceptor conjugated organic polymers. This work examines how backbone curvature, acceptor identity, chain length, and molecular structure affect electronic properties, singlet–triplet gaps, and absorption spectra in conjugated polymers.

Read the Scientific Reports paper (2023), DOI: 10.1038/s41598-023-48468-0

Stimuli-responsive photoluminescent copper(I) halides

Stimuli-responsive photoluminescent copper(I) halides for scintillation, anticounterfeiting, and light-emitting diode applications. This collaborative study reports low-dimensional copper(I) halides with bright emission from self-trapped excitons. The materials show near-unity photoluminescence quantum yields, room-temperature radioluminescence, and chemical and thermal switching, linking their structure and excited-state distortions to potential applications in scintillation, sensing, information storage, anticounterfeiting, and solid-state lighting.

The reported compounds are interconvertible through chemical stimuli or reverse crystallization, while their luminescence can be switched on and off thermally. These results illustrate how low-dimensional halide chemistry can combine optical brightness with stimuli responsiveness.

Read the Advanced Theory and Simulations paper, DOI: 10.1002/agt2.602

Research directions

  • Investigate lead-free and double perovskites and related semiconductors.
  • Use GW and Bethe–Salpeter equation methods to study absorption, excitons, and emission.
  • Study defects, stability, quantum confinement, and stimuli-responsive emission in optoelectronic materials.

Self-consistent GW theory

My background includes self-consistent GW methods for semiconductors and relativistic coupled-cluster calculations for atomic properties and electric dipole moments. Across these areas, I focus on connecting accurate many-body electronic structure with measurable materials properties.

Traditional Kohn–Sham density functional theory with local exchange-correlation functionals can substantially underestimate semiconductor band gaps because of limitations in the treatment of quasiparticle excitations.

Fully self-consistent GW methods, including scGW and QSGW approaches, offer routes beyond these limitations. My work examines approximations that reduce computational cost while retaining an accurate description of bulk semiconducting systems.

Tagged work: Analysis of diagonal G and subspace W approximations within fully self-consistent GW calculations for bulk semiconducting systems.
