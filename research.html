01 / ELECTRONIC-STRUCTURE METHODS

Self-interaction correction and localized orbitals

Self-interaction error can distort the description of localized electronic states, stretched bonds, and transition states. My work examines these error sources and develops methods within density-functional theory that retain an accurate, physically meaningful orbital picture.

A central direction is a Wannier-first approach that constructs localized, Wannier-like orbitals directly in real space using Gaussian basis functions, reducing dependence on conventional canonical Bloch-like states during the self-consistent cycle.

FEATURED PAPER

A Wannier-first formulation for extended systems

This real-space formulation constructs variational Wannier-like functions self-consistently within a finite Wannier domain. The orbitals provide the occupied space and are used to build the charge density, electrostatic potential, and per-cell energy, while band structures can be recovered afterward from a Bloch-like Gaussian basis.

The framework naturally accommodates combined translation–rotation (screw) symmetries, making it suitable for chiral and helical systems with finite twist angles. Demonstrations on linear and twisted chains, graphene, AA graphite, and helically stacked graphite show close agreement with reference periodic calculations and establish a foundation for orbital-dependent functionals such as Perdew–Zunger self-interaction correction.

02 / REACTION BARRIERS

Analyzing PZ-SIC beyond the LSDA

Incorporating self-interaction corrections can substantially improve density-functional predictions of chemical reaction barrier heights. In this work, I performed an orbital-by-orbital analysis of Perdew–Zunger self-interaction corrections within FLOSIC.

The analysis follows the reactant (R), transition-state (TS), and product (P) configurations. Across all three functionals, the dominant contribution to the SIC of barrier heights comes from orbitals whose localization changes along the reaction coordinate.

XC/H as an orbital diagnostic

The ratio of self-exchange–correlation energy to self-Hartree energy, XC/H, measures the one-electron self-interaction error of an orbital.

XC/H = 1.0 means exact cancellation and therefore no contribution to the FLOSIC correction. Larger values are associated with stretched or strongly lobed orbitals.

03 / CATALYSIS AND ENERGY

Reliable reaction energetics

I use first-principles calculations to investigate catalytic mechanisms and structure-property relationships in transition-metal phosphides and carbides, single-atom catalysts, and doped two-dimensional materials.

Reaction barriers involving stretched bonds and hydrogen transfer provide a connection between electronic-structure accuracy and catalytic predictions.

Research directions

  • Screen transition-metal, bimetallic, and 2D catalyst platforms using DFT and targeted self-interaction corrections.
  • Identify chemical regimes where localized states and stretched-bond intermediates require improved electronic-structure treatments.
  • Explore solvent and electrolyte effects using ab initio molecular dynamics and quantum continuum approaches.
04 / OPTOELECTRONICS

Electronic and optical response

My research links electronic structure to optical behavior in perovskites and related semiconductors, hybrid halides, and donor–acceptor conjugated polymers. Collaboration with experimental groups connects computed structures and excitations to measurable optical response.

Research directions

  • Investigate lead-free and double perovskites and related semiconductors.
  • Use GW and Bethe–Salpeter equation methods to study absorption, excitons, and emission.
  • Study defects, stability, and quantum confinement in optoelectronic materials.
05 / MANY-BODY THEORY

Self-consistent GW theory

My background includes self-consistent GW methods for semiconductors and relativistic coupled-cluster calculations for atomic properties and electric dipole moments. Across these areas, I develop computational approaches that connect accurate many-body descriptions with practical materials calculations.

Tagged work: Analysis of diagonal G and subspace W approximations within fully self-consistent GW calculations for bulk semiconducting systems.
