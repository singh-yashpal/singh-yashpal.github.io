01 / ELECTRONIC-STRUCTURE METHODS

Self-interaction correction and localized orbitals

Self-interaction error can distort the description of localized electronic states, stretched bonds, and transition states. My work examines these error sources and develops methods within the Fermi–Löwdin orbital self-interaction correction (FLO-SIC) framework for periodic materials.

A central direction is a Wannier-first approach that constructs localized, Wannier-like orbitals directly in real space using Gaussian basis functions, reducing dependence on conventional Bloch-state workflows.

Research directions

  • Extend periodic FLOSIC to magnets, chiral, helical, and low-symmetry systems.
  • Evaluate Perdew–Zunger self-interaction corrections for stretched bonds, charged defects, and localized excitations.
  • Develop automated workflows and benchmark datasets for systematic validation.
02 / REACTION BARRIERS

Analyzing PZ-SIC beyond the LSDA

Incorporating self-interaction corrections can substantially improve density-functional predictions of chemical reaction barrier heights. In this work, I performed an orbital-by-orbital analysis of FLOSIC corrections for three semi-local density-functional approximations on the three lowest rungs of Jacob’s ladder, following four representative reactions from the BH76 benchmark set.

The analysis follows the reactant (R), transition-state (TS), and product (P) configurations. Across all three functionals, the dominant contribution to the SIC of barrier heights comes from stretched-bond orbitals that emerge near the transition state.

XC/H as an orbital diagnostic

The ratio of self-exchange–correlation energy to self-Hartree energy, XC/H, measures the one-electron self-interaction error of an orbital.

XC/H = 1.0 means exact cancellation and therefore no contribution to the FLOSIC correction. Larger values are associated with stretched or strongly lobed orbitals.

Evolution along the reaction path

Comparing XC/H for corresponding orbitals in R, TS, and P identifies which orbitals drive the SIC of reaction energies and barrier heights.

This provides a direct connection between the electronic structure of stretched bonds and the accuracy of predicted reaction kinetics.

03 / CATALYSIS AND ENERGY

Reliable reaction energetics

I use first-principles calculations to investigate catalytic mechanisms and structure-property relationships in transition-metal phosphides and carbides, single-atom catalysts, and doped two-dimensional materials. Applications include oxygen reduction, oxygen evolution, hydrogen evolution, and selective hydrogenation.

Reaction barriers involving stretched bonds and hydrogen transfer provide a connection between electronic-structure accuracy and catalytic predictions.

Research directions

  • Screen transition-metal, bimetallic, and 2D catalyst platforms using DFT and targeted self-interaction corrections.
  • Identify chemical regimes where localized states and stretched-bond intermediates require improved electronic-structure treatments.
  • Explore solvent and electrolyte effects using ab initio molecular dynamics and quantum continuum approaches.
04 / OPTOELECTRONICS

Electronic and optical response

My research links electronic structure to optical behavior in perovskites and related semiconductors, hybrid halides, and donor–acceptor conjugated polymers. Collaboration with experimental groups connects calculations to materials selection and the interpretation of optical measurements.

Research directions

  • Investigate lead-free and double perovskites and related semiconductors.
  • Use GW and Bethe–Salpeter equation methods to study absorption, excitons, and emission.
  • Study defects, stability, and quantum confinement in optoelectronic materials.
05 / MANY-BODY THEORY

Self-consistent GW theory

My background includes self-consistent GW methods for semiconductors and relativistic coupled-cluster calculations for atomic properties and electric dipole moments. Across these areas, I focus on balancing electronic-structure accuracy with memory use and computational cost.

Tagged work: Analysis of diagonal G and subspace W approximations within fully self-consistent GW calculations for bulk semiconducting systems.

Research directions described here include ongoing interests and proposed extensions, rather than completed project outcomes.
