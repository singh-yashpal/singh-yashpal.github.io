Computational toolkit

Skills & Methods

Physics-based modeling supported by scientific programming and high-performance computing.

Electronic-structure theory

Density functional theory; Perdew-Zunger self-interaction correction; Fermi-Löwdin orbital self-interaction correction; coupled-cluster theory; GW approximation; Wannier functions and localized orbitals; periodic boundary conditions.

Atomistic simulation

Ab initio molecular dynamics; transition-state and reaction-barrier modeling; charge localization; quasiparticle properties; electronic and optical spectra; structure-property analysis.

Materials applications

Catalysis and electrocatalysis; energy-storage materials; perovskites and hybrid halides; semiconductors; 2D materials; donor-acceptor polymers; singlet-triplet and excited states.

Scientific software and HPC

Python, C, Fortran, MPI, Linux, workflow scripting, and high-performance clusters. Method development emphasizes scalable simulations and efficient memory use.

Electronic-structure software

FLOSIC, VASP, CP2K, Quantum ESPRESSO, JDFTx, Q-Chem, BerkeleyGW, and related workflows.

Working across theory and experiment

I connect atomistic models, spectra, reaction mechanisms, and electronic structure to materials performance. My experience includes collaborative studies with experimentalists and theorists to interpret observations, evaluate models, and identify useful computational directions.
