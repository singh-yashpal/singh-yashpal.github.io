About me

I am a computational physicist and quantum chemist with a background in electronic-structure theory, first-principles materials modeling, and quantum many-body methods. My research explores how electron correlation, localization, and bonding influence the behavior of physical and chemical systems.

I work with density functional theory, self-interaction corrections, Wannier and localized-orbital approaches, GW, and coupled-cluster theory. My experience spans atomic physics, low-dimensional materials, electrocatalysis, energy storage, and optoelectronic materials.

A central goal of my work is to improve the predictive reliability of computational modeling so that materials and reaction mechanisms can be understood more effectively before experimental synthesis or testing.

Research interests

  • Electronic-structure methods: Developing and applying approaches to describe electron correlation and reduce self-interaction errors.
  • Localized orbitals and periodic systems: Exploring Wannier-based and localized-orbital frameworks for extended materials.
  • Materials and reactions: First-principles modeling of catalysts, energy materials, semiconductors, and optoelectronic systems.
  • Scientific computing: Building reproducible simulation workflows with Python, C, Fortran, and high-performance computing.
