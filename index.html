About

I am a computational physicist and quantum chemist specializing in electronic-structure theory, first-principles materials modeling, and quantum many-body methods.

My research focuses on developing and applying advanced computational approaches, including density functional theory, self-interaction corrections, Wannier-based methods, GW, and coupled-cluster theory, to study electron correlation, localization, bonding, and material properties.

Research

My work spans atomic physics, low-dimensional materials, electrocatalysis, energy storage, optoelectronics, semiconductor materials, and emerging quantum technologies.

A central goal of my research is to improve the predictive reliability of computational materials modeling so that complex materials can be designed and understood more efficiently before experimental synthesis or testing.

Selected Publications

  • Computational exploration of borophane-supported single transition metal atoms as potential oxygen reduction and evolution electrocatalysts.
  • Longitudinal unzipping of 2D transition metal dichalcogenides.
  • Stimuli-responsive photoluminescent copper(I) halides for scintillation, anticounterfeiting, and LED applications.
  • Rigorous limits on CP-violating coupling constants from the electric dipole moment of mercury-199.

Experience

My research experience includes work at Central Michigan University, Mississippi State University, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, KAIST, and the Physical Research Laboratory.
